What Is a Flood?

A flood happens when water covers land that is normally dry. Rivers can overflow after heavy monsoon rain, when drains are blocked, or when water is released from upstream. Floods can come slowly over days or very suddenly, and they are one of the most common natural disasters in Myanmar.

Taungoo is a town in the Bago Region near the Sittaung River, so households close to the riverbank can be affected when the water level rises during the rainy season. Knowing the risks and planning ahead helps people stay safe.

After the Flood: Basic Solutions

When the water goes down, careful action reduces health risks and long-term damage.

Wait for the all-clear

Return home only when local authorities say it is safe. Avoid floodwater, which can hide sharp objects and electric hazards.

Switch off power

Do not use electricity or gas appliances until they are checked by a qualified person.

Clean and disinfect

Remove mud, wash walls and floors, and dry rooms well to prevent mould and disease.

Drink safe water

Boil drinking water or use purification tablets. Throw away food that touched floodwater.

Document damage

Take photos and make a list of losses so you can ask for help from local relief groups.

Help each other

Check on neighbours, the elderly and children, and share tools, food and information.

Simple Flood Techniques

Low-cost ideas that households can use before and during a flood.

Raise valuables

Keep documents, electronics and food on high shelves or upper floors in waterproof bags.

Sandbags and barriers

Place sandbags at doors and low openings to slow water from entering.

Keep drains clear

Remove rubbish from drains and ditches around the home so water can flow away.

Go-bag ready

Pack water, food, medicine, a torch, radio and copies of IDs in one easy-to-grab bag.
